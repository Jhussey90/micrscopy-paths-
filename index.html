How microscopy contrast methods use light

Brightfield

Contrast is mainly from absorption and scattering.

  1. 1Illuminate. The lamp sends light through the field diaphragm and condenser.
  2. 2Interact. Light passes through the specimen; some is absorbed or scattered.
  3. 3Collect. The objective gathers the remaining transmitted light.
  4. 4Form image. Tube optics project the image to the camera or eye.

Phase contrast

Contrast comes from interference between reference and specimen-altered light.

  1. 1Shape illumination. The annulus creates a hollow cone or ring of illumination.
  2. 2Change phase. The specimen delays or advances light according to its optical path length.
  3. 3Shift reference. A phase ring changes the phase and often the amplitude of undeviated light.
  4. 4Interfere. Reference and diffracted waves recombine; their interference appears as brightness contrast.

Differential interference contrast (DIC)

Contrast comes from the optical-path difference between nearby points.

  1. 1Polarize. The first polarizer prepares the illumination for the DIC prisms.
  2. 2Split and shear. A prism makes two polarized beams that sample slightly separated points.
  3. 3Accumulate path difference. Thickness or refractive-index changes affect the two beams differently.
  4. 4Recombine. A second prism and analyzer let the beams interfere, emphasizing directional changes in optical path.

Darkfield

Contrast comes from light scattered into the objective.

  1. 1Block central light. A stop removes direct, on-axis illumination.
  2. 2Illuminate obliquely. The condenser sends a hollow cone of light toward the sample.
  3. 3Scatter. Specimen features redirect some oblique light into the objective.
  4. 4Image. Scattered light appears bright; unscattered background light is mostly excluded.

Fluorescence

The image is formed from light emitted by fluorescent labels.

  1. 1Select excitation. The excitation filter passes wavelengths that excite the fluorophore.
  2. 2Direct excitation. The dichroic mirror reflects excitation light through the objective to the specimen.
  3. 3Emit light. Fluorophores absorb energy and emit light at a longer wavelength.
  4. 4Separate and detect. Emission returns through the objective, passes the dichroic and emission filter, and reaches the detector.

These are explanatory schematics rather than scale drawings. Actual microscope layouts vary. Phase contrast and DIC use interference; brightfield and darkfield rely on transmitted or scattered light; fluorescence detects wavelength-separated emission from fluorophores.
